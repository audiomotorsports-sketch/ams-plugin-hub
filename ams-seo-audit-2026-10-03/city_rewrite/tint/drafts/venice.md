## What Venice drivers bring us

- **Car window tint for cars parked outside.** Film that is cut to the exact glass and tucked to the gasket does not lift at the edge when the car sits in damp air for months. That edge is where a rushed job starts peeling.
- **Privacy window tinting for vans.** Camper vans and work vans get the side and rear glass darkened so nobody can see the build-out inside. Van glass is large and flat, so it is laid in full sheets with no seams.
- **Ceramic window tint for heat.** Solar Ceramic cuts the infrared load so the cabin is not an oven after an afternoon at the beach. The front doors stay at the legal shade.
- **Tesla window tint.** The one-piece rear glass on a Model 3 or Model Y takes a single sheet shaped to the curve with heat before it goes on.

## Questions from Venice drivers

### Will window tint hold up on a car that never sees a garage in Venice?

Yes, if it is installed properly. The film itself is built for outdoor life; what fails is a poorly sealed edge. We cut each piece on the glass and press the edges down to the seal, so moisture and salt have nowhere to get under.

### Can you tint the back of a camper van so the inside is hidden?

That is one of the most common van requests. A dark Solar film on the rear and side glass stops anyone from seeing gear, beds or tools inside, while you can still see out from the driver's seat. Large panes go on as single sheets.

### Should a Venice driver pay more for ceramic film instead of standard Solar?

If heat bothers you, yes. Solar Ceramic rejects far more infrared energy than standard film at the same shade, so the car starts cooler and the air conditioning catches up sooner. For privacy alone, Solar does the job for less.
