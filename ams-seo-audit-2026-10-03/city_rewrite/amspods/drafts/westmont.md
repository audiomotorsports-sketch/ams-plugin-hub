## What Westmont riders bring us

**Audio set up for distance.** Harshness from a clipping amplifier is what tires you out. The amp is tuned with headroom to spare so the music stays clean for hours at freeway speed, and the pods are aimed so you are not fighting the wind with the volume.

**AMS lower-fairing speaker pods.** Chrome or gloss, built in Carson, bracket and screws included. They restore the midrange the fairing speakers lose once the windshield is doing its job.

**Amplifier installation on the bike.** Fused at the battery; fairing amp with a tank pull on the Glides, saddlebag amp on a carpeted board with an AMS quick disconnect on the Road King.

**Speaker replacement.** Stock speakers swapped for weatherproof Hertz Neo units that stay clear when they are loud.

**Bag-lid bass.** Cut lids with carpeted spacers, inverted speakers and soldered quick disconnects, so the bags carry the low end and still close.

### Why does a bagger stereo get tiring after a couple of hours?

Distortion. A radio or amplifier pushed past its limit clips, and that harshness wears on your ears long before the volume does. Clean power and pods aimed at the rider let you ride at a moderate level that still beats the wind.

### Do I need pods if my fairing speakers are new?

For long freeway rides, probably. Fairing speakers sit up high in the wind; lower-fairing pods fire at you from below it. The combination is what lets you hear the music clearly at a volume you can live with for a whole day.

### What does a Westmont rider on a Road King get?

Pods for the midrange, cut bag lids with inverted speakers for the bass, and an amplifier in a saddlebag on a carpeted board with a quick disconnect. The sound is aimed at the rider, which is what matters without a fairing.
