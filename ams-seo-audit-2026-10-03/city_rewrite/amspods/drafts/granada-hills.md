## What Granada Hills riders bring us

**A sensible first step.** Not every bike needs everything at once. The shop looks at the year, the radio and how the bike is ridden, then recommends an order: often speakers and an amp first, pods next, bag subwoofers when the budget allows.

**AMS pods.** Built in the shop in chrome or gloss with the crash-bar bracket and screws included. For a new bagger owner this is the upgrade that changes the ride the most, because the sound arrives at the rider instead of the windshield.

**Amplifier installation and tuning on the bike.** The factory radio powers four small speakers badly. A proper amp, tuned with the engine running, is what makes replacement speakers and pods worth the money.

**Head unit installation.** Older factory radios have no real preamp output. Diamond Audio, Sony and Soundstream Harley radios are stocked for the bikes that need one.

### What should a first-time bagger owner upgrade first?

Start with the signal chain: a radio with real outputs if yours lacks one, an amplifier, then speakers. AMS pods come next because they add a second front stage close to the rider. Doing it in that order means nothing gets bought twice.

### Is Granada Hills too far to ride for a first upgrade?

No. Riders routinely cover far more than 33.7 miles for this work, and a first upgrade is one planned visit. Text the year and model, let the pods get built over about three weeks if you are adding them, then ride in once for the install and tuning.

### Do I need to know anything about audio before I call?

Only how you ride and roughly what you want to spend. Nick asks about the year, the radio and whether the bike is a Glide or a Road King, then quotes a plan at the shop. There is no obligation to do the whole list in one go.
