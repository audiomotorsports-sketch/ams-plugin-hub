## Amplifier questions from Lakewood riders

### Does a Street Glide really need an amplifier for AMS pods?

For full volume at freeway speed, yes. The stock radio can drive the pods, but it runs out of clean power early and starts to distort when you turn it up into the wind. An amplifier gives the pods and the fairing speakers headroom, so the sound stays clear at 70 mph.

### Where does the amplifier go on a Road King with no fairing?

In a saddlebag, with an AMS quick disconnect so the bag still comes off. The power lead runs from the battery through a fuse, and the remote turn-on comes from the head unit. On the Glides the amp lives inside the fairing instead, behind the inner panel.

### What does tuning on the bike actually involve?

We set the amplifier gains to the head unit's real output, pick crossover points so the pods handle mids and highs while the bass stays where it belongs, and balance the channels with the engine running. Then you ride it and we adjust again if the road tells us something different.

### Is a Lakewood rider going to wait all day for an amp install?

Plan on the better part of a day for an amplifier, pods and tuning together, so the morning slot is the smart one. Many Lakewood riders drop the bike and get picked up, since it is only 7.7 miles. A pods-only install is shorter, and riders often wait at the shop for that.

### Can I bring my own amplifier from Lakewood and have it installed?

You can, and we will tell you up front whether it is a good match for the pods and your head unit. Labor and wiring are quoted on the bike. If the amp is underpowered or the wrong channel count, we say so before anything is pulled apart, not after.
