## Wiring questions from Manhattan Beach riders

### Why does my bagger stereo cut out when I hit a bump?

Almost always a wiring problem: a loose ground, a push-on connector that worked free, or a splice taped instead of crimped. The fix is to run the harness properly, crimp and cover each connection, and ground the amp to clean metal. That is the part of a bagger audio installation nobody sees and everybody hears.

### How is the pod wiring routed on a Street Glide?

From the amplifier in the fairing, down the frame with the factory harness, and into each lower through the pod bracket. The wire is sleeved where it passes near the engine and the crash bar. Nothing is visible when the lowers are back on, and nothing chafes.

### Will the fairing wiring stay dry on a Manhattan Beach morning?

Yes. Connections are made inside the fairing or the pod housing, not out in the open, and the amp sits above anything that puddles. Salt air is part of riding near the coast and the speakers we recommend, Hertz Neo in the pods, are built for weather.

### Can a Manhattan Beach rider get a wiring repair without buying anything?

Yes. If the parts are good and only the install is bad, we rewire what is there, quoted on the bike after we look inside the fairing. Riders bring us a lot of systems that just need to be done once, properly. Pods or an amp can be added later if you want more.

### How long does the bike stay at the shop for a full rewire and pods?

A rewire with pods and an amp is most of a day. Ride in for a morning appointment, hand over the keys, and pick the bike up tuned by late afternoon. A straightforward pods install is quicker, and riders often wait at the shop for it.
