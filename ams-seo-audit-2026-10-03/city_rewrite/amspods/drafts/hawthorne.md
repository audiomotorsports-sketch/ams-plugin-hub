## What Hawthorne riders bring us

**Systems set up for hours, not minutes.** The amp is tuned for clean headroom instead of a loud demo in the parking lot. That is what keeps the music listenable after two hundred miles and keeps the speakers from cooking.

**AMS pods.** Lower-fairing speaker pods built in-house, chrome or gloss, with the crash-bar bracket and screws. They put the sound close to the rider so the fairing does not have to be driven hard.

**Hertz Neo speakers.** The shop's standard choice for the pods: compact neodymium magnets, weatherproof build and clear output when loud for a long time.

**Amplifier installation.** Fairing-mounted on the Glides, saddlebag-mounted with a quick disconnect on a Road King, powered from the battery through a fuse with soldered connections.

## Questions Hawthorne riders ask

### What makes a bagger system hold up on a long ride?

Headroom and placement. An amplifier tuned below clipping does not heat the speakers, and pods near the rider mean the fairing is not pushed to its limit for hours. Weatherproof speakers finish it off, because a long ride includes sun, heat and sometimes rain.

### Is Hawthorne a short enough trip to drop the bike and come back?

Yes. At about 8.0 miles it is an easy drop-off in the morning and pickup before closing. Many Hawthorne riders do exactly that for an amp and pods install, which usually takes most of a day.

### Do I need a subwoofer in the bags for highway riding?

Not everyone does, but riders who want the low end that wind strips away usually add one. The saddlebag lids are cut for inverted speakers on carpeted spacers, wired with soldered quick disconnects, and the bag stays usable.

### Will a long-ride system be too loud around town?

No. A properly tuned amp has range; the volume knob still works. The difference is that at highway speed the system has reserve instead of distortion, so Hawthorne riders get a clean sound at both ends.
