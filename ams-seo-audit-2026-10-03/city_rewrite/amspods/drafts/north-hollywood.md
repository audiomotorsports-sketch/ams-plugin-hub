## Long-ride questions from North Hollywood

### Why does my fairing stereo sound fine in town and vanish on the freeway?

Wind. Above about 50 mph the air moving over the fairing masks the midrange from the upper speakers. Pods mounted low on the crash bar sit outside that blast and aim at you, so the vocals and guitars come back without turning the volume to the stop.

### Will an amplifier overheat on a long summer ride through the desert?

Not if it is mounted and tuned right. The amp goes where air can move around it, the gains are set to the head unit's real output rather than maxed, and the wiring is sized for the current. An amp that goes into protect on a grade is usually one that was installed hot and cranked.

### Is North Hollywood too far to ride for a pods install?

No. Riders have come from San Jose by phone and from Temecula on the bike, so 24.7 miles is a normal appointment. Book a morning slot, ride down, and the bike is tuned and ready in the afternoon.

### Can I keep my factory head unit on a Street Glide and still get long-ride sound?

Often, yes. The factory deck can feed an amplifier through a signal converter, and the pods and fairing speakers do the rest. If you want more sources or cleaner output, a head unit installation is the next step, quoted on the bike.

### What should I ask for if I ride two-up most of the time?

Tell us when you book. Pods aim at the rider, so for a passenger we look at bag-lid speakers and the rear channel balance during the tune. The goal is sound both of you hear at speed, not a system that only works in the front seat.
