# First Bagger Audio Upgrade for Altadena Harley Riders

A new-to-you Street Glide, Road Glide or Road King usually comes with a stereo somebody gave up on. ## What Altadena riders bring us

**A plan before parts.** Nick looks at the year, the radio and the bike, then recommends what to do first and what can wait. A rider who wants to spread the cost can do it in stages without buying anything twice.

**Amplifier installation and tuning.** The factory radio drives small speakers poorly. A real amp, set with the engine running, is the step that makes everything after it worth doing.

**Speaker replacement.** Weatherproof speakers in the fairing, and Hertz Neo units in the pods when those go on, so the whole bike sounds like one system.

## Questions Altadena riders ask

### I just bought my first bagger; where do I start with the audio?

Start by finding out whether the factory radio has usable outputs. If it does, an amplifier and better speakers come first. If it does not, a Harley-specific head unit goes in before anything else. Pods follow once the front stage is right.

### Can an Altadena rider spread the upgrade across two visits?

Yes. The wiring is planned for the full system on the first visit, with amp channels and quick disconnects left ready for pods. When the pods are built, about three weeks after ordering, the second trip is a short plug-in and tune.

### Do I need to buy the parts before I ride in?

No. Radios from Diamond Audio, Sony and Soundstream are in the shop, speakers are stocked, and the pods are built in-house. Bring the bike, tell Nick how you ride and what you want to spend, and the parts are matched to the bike.

### What does the first visit cost from Altadena?

The amplifier, speakers and any radio are quoted at the shop after Nick sees the bike, so there is no fixed number for a first visit. AMS pods are the one set price: from $649 a pair as of October 2026. Payment plan available, ask Nick.

### Will the finished install look stock?

Yes. The amp hides in the fairing or a saddlebag, the pods use the correct bracket on the crash bar, and the wiring is soldered, tied down and routed under the tank. Nothing dangles and nothing is drilled that does not need to be.
