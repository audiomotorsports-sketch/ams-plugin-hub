## San Pedro amplifier questions

### What keeps an amplifier installation from adding alternator whine?

A proper ground and a clean signal path. We sand the ground point to bare metal, keep the ground lead short, route power and signal wires on opposite sides of the car and set the gain correctly. If the factory head unit is noisy, a line-output converter with ground-loop handling solves it.

### Is a factory head unit enough to run an aftermarket amplifier in a San Pedro car?

Yes. A line-output converter turns the speaker-level signal into a clean low-level feed for the amplifier, and a remote turn-on is derived so the amp switches on with the radio. You keep the stock look and the factory controls while gaining real power to the speakers.

### How big an amplifier do I need for door speakers plus a subwoofer?

Usually a four-channel amp for the front and rear speakers and a mono amp for the sub, or a five-channel unit that does both. The right size depends on the speakers and the sub you choose; we match the amplifier to the load rather than selling the biggest one on the shelf.

### Can I drive up from San Pedro and wait while the amp goes in?

For a swap onto existing wiring, yes, that is a wait-inside job. A first-time amplifier installation with a new power run from the battery, a ground, signal cables and a tune is a longer job, and dropping the car for the day lets us hide the wiring properly.

### What else gets checked during an amplifier installation?

We confirm the alternator and battery are up to the extra draw, verify the fuse sizing on the main power lead, check speaker impedance so the amp is not run below its rating and then tune by ear and meter. The car leaves with clean, balanced sound and no buzzes.
