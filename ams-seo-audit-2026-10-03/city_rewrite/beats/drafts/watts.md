## Watts speaker and stereo questions

### How do I know my speakers need replacing and not just the radio?

Crackle or buzz at moderate volume, a speaker that cuts in and out, or a dull sound on one side usually means a torn cone or a failed surround. We test each speaker at the shop before recommending speaker replacement, and if the radio is the real problem we say so.

### Will new speakers sound better on the factory radio in a Watts car?

Yes, noticeably. Good replacement speakers are more efficient and handle the factory power more cleanly than worn originals. If you want them to play loud without strain, an amplifier installation is the next step, but it is not required to hear the difference.

### Does speaker replacement mean cutting into the doors or the wiring?

No. Vehicle-specific adapters position the speaker in the factory hole, and plug-in harnesses connect to the stock wiring. The door panel goes back on with the clips intact. Nothing is cut, so the car could be returned to stock if it were ever needed.

### Can I combine speaker replacement with a head unit installation in one trip from Watts?

That is the most common pairing. The head unit goes in with a dash kit and harness, the speakers go in while the doors are open, and the whole system is tested together. One appointment, one drive down from Watts, and the car comes back finished.

### Why bring the car to Carson instead of a quick install nearby?

Speakers that are screwed straight to a door with no adapter or seal sound thin and rattle within weeks. Doing the mounting, sealing and wiring correctly is the service you are paying for, and it lasts. Done once, done right beats paying twice.
