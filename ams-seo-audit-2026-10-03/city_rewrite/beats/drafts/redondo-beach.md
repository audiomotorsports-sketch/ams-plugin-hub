## Redondo Beach subwoofer questions

### Will a subwoofer take up my whole trunk in a Redondo Beach hatchback?

It does not have to. Compact loaded boxes and low-profile enclosures leave most of the cargo area free, and the amplifier can mount against a side wall or under a seat. We size the box to the vehicle and to how much bass you actually want, then anchor it so it does not slide.

### What is included in a subwoofer installation at the shop?

The loaded box, an amplifier matched to it, a fused power wire from the battery, a bare-metal ground, a remote turn-on lead, signal from the head unit or a line-output converter on a factory radio, and a tune. The trunk and rear deck are checked for rattles before the car goes back.

### Can I keep the factory radio and still add a sub?

Yes. A line-output converter takes signal from the factory speaker wires and feeds the amplifier, and the remote turn-on is derived so the amp powers up with the car. The stock head unit and all its controls stay. A head unit installation later can feed the same amp directly.

### How do you keep the bass from sounding boomy?

The crossover on the amplifier is set so the sub plays only the low notes, the gain is matched to the head unit output instead of maxed, and the box is matched to the sub. The result is bass you feel under the music rather than a one-note thump that drowns the doors.

### Is Redondo Beach close enough to wait while the sub goes in?

For a box-only hookup to an existing amplifier, yes, that is a wait-inside job. A full subwoofer installation with a new amplifier and wiring takes longer, and a morning drop-off lets the wiring be hidden properly. Either way it is about 7.2 miles back to Redondo Beach.
