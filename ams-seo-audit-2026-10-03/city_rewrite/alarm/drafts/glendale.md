### What makes your Viper alarm installation different from a quick install elsewhere?

Placement and wiring. The module is not stacked on the fuse box, the siren is not where a hand reaches it from the wheel well, and every connection is soldered and taped rather than crimped and left to vibrate loose. Those choices decide whether the alarm can be found and silenced in a minute or not.

### Does the Viper 3108V come with a starter kill, and what does that do?

Yes. The starter kill is a relay that keeps the starter circuit open until the alarm is disarmed, so even a thief who gets inside and defeats the factory immobilizer finds the engine will not crank. We place that relay away from the obvious spots, which is why the $269 installed price as of October 2026 covers real labor.

### Can the Viper alarm be upgraded later to add remote start or phone control?

Usually, yes. Many Viper systems accept an add-on remote start or a phone module later, and when we do the first install we leave the harness ready for it. Tell us at the first visit that you may want remote start and we will plan the wiring so the second job is shorter.

### How do I keep the alarm from false-triggering in a Glendale parking garage?

Sensitivity is set on your car, not left at the factory default, and we show you how to adjust it. A two-stage shock sensor chirps at a light bump and only goes to full siren on a hard strike, so a door tapped in a tight garage gives a warning rather than a two-minute wail.

### Is Glendale too far to come to Carson for a car alarm?

No. It is about an hour each way, and the work takes a half day, so a morning appointment gets you home with the job finished. People drive farther than that to get a stereo installed; a security system that decides whether you keep your car is worth the same trip.
